A LITTLE MORE✳ROOM TO FLOURISH
Good work.
Human connection.
More of what matters.

The people. The learning. The chance to try something new, contribute something meaningful, and occasionally surprise yourself.

There’s a lot to love about working. There are also days when we lose sight of it.

The Joy of Working explores how we find more enjoyment in our working lives—and how, as leaders, we create environments where others can find it too.

Through speaking, workshops, and coaching, I help people understand what brings them alive at work and make intentional choices about what comes next.

01 / THE IDEA

What helps you
come alive
at work?

✳It’s personal.

For some of us, it’s the people. For others, it’s solving a difficult problem, creating something, learning a new skill, or having the freedom to get on with it.

We don’t all find enjoyment in the same things. And what we need can change throughout our lives.

Finding more joy at work starts with paying attention: to who you are, what matters to you, and the conditions that help you contribute at your best.

Then comes the practical part. What can you shape? What are you willing to adapt to? What conversation needs to happen? And when might you need a different environment?

You don’t have to love every Monday or every meeting.

But it’s worth asking what would make your working life more enjoyable—and what you can do about it.

02 / A PRACTICAL APPROACH

Understand yourself.
Understand each other.
Do something with what you learn.

01 ↗

Know yourself

Get clearer about what energizes you, what matters to you, and what helps you do good work. Notice the expectations you’ve absorbed along the way—and decide which still make sense.

02 ↗

Choose intentionally

Growth involves change. So does working with other people. Learn to recognize the difference between an adjustment that helps you grow and a compromise that costs you too much.

03 ↗

Create the conditions

As a leader, get curious about the individuals on your team. Understand their strengths, motivations, and aspirations. Look for practical ways to connect what people need with what the work requires.

↳ There will be tradeoffs. There will be constraints. Understanding them gives us a better place to start.

We’re all a work in progress. Which changes are helping you become who you want to be?

We learn to read the room. Adjust our tone. Take feedback. Speak up—or hold back.

Over a working lifetime, those edits add up.

Some make us more capable, considerate, and effective. Others become habits we no longer question. Sometimes we become so good at fitting in that we lose touch with what we bring.

A Million Edits is an upbeat, thoughtful exploration of how we adapt, grow, and make more intentional choices about who we’re becoming.

Together, we’ll explore:

  • Which edits help us grow—and which deserve another look.
  • How to adapt without disappearing.
  • How leaders can support development while making room for different personalities and perspectives.
  • Where we can bring more curiosity, enjoyment, and intention into our working lives.

Expect stories, humour, recognition, and a few questions that stay with you after the room clears.

05 / A FEW GOOD QUESTIONS

You might
be wondering.

?
Is this about expecting people to be happy all the time?+

No. Difficult days, frustration, and demanding work are part of life. The goal is to understand what makes work more fulfilling and enjoyable, while being honest about what gets in the way.

What if the workplace itself is the problem?+

Sometimes meaningful change requires action from leaders or the organization. Sometimes an individual needs to consider a different environment. Personal choices matter, and so do the conditions people work in.

Can a leader realistically give everyone what they want?+

No—and pretending otherwise doesn’t help anyone. Leaders can understand their people, explain constraints, set clear expectations, and explore where flexibility is possible.

Can a session be shaped around our team or event?+

Yes. We’ll start with your audience, what they’re experiencing, and what you want the session to help them think about or do differently. Then we’ll agree on the focus and format.
